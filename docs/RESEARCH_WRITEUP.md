# A Pre-Registered Search for Equity Alpha on a Retail Budget

*What a systematic trading platform can and cannot find with public data, and how to avoid fooling yourself.*

*All file paths below are relative to the repository root. Study documents live in `research/medallion_style_alpha_search/`; where a bare filename is cited, that is its directory.*

## Abstract

Between 18 June and 14 July 2026 I directed a systematic search for deployable equity alpha using data available to a retail researcher: prices and volumes, Fama–French factor loadings, SEC EDGAR fundamentals, SEC Form 4 insider filings, and one month of paid survivorship-free fundamentals (~£60). Every study from the learned-combination stage onward ran through a single validation pipeline (purged walk-forward with embargo, a Deflated Sharpe Ratio gate at 0.95, and Probability of Backtest Overfitting), with fixed feature sets, honest trial accounting, and a default-deny decision rule; the insider study and the closing dev/confirm programme were additionally pre-registered. The answer is a properly executed negative result: across five data sources, no candidate survived deflation. The strongest result (DSR 0.905 on 21,916 survivorship-free names) was subsequently diagnosed as very likely the same untradeable micro-cap artefact class exposed directly in the development window. A real but tiny ordering edge (rank-IC ≈ +0.013, development window) exists in clean fundamentals and cannot be monetised by this construction at honest costs. The pre-registered confirmation test was deliberately left unfired.

## 1. Motivation

The programme began with a target that most retail trading projects hold as an article of faith: high returns at low risk, in this project's case a 30%-a-year target I had set for it at the outset. The governing plan demoted that number to a hypothesis before any paid data was touched: "This plan does not promise 30%/year. No honest plan can... Faith belongs in that process, not in a number", and, more bluntly, "Reliable low-risk 30%/year is not on the table" (`docs/project-control/DATA_EDGE_PLAN.md` L9–11, L61). The same document fixes the ordering that shaped everything after it: leverage scales an edge you already have, so validate first and scale second (L19–20).

The reason for this stance is the base rate. A retail backtester who tries enough signals will find one that looks excellent in-sample; the finding is usually a property of the search, not the market. The project had already lived this. Its early engine produced a market-beating backtest on 8 hand-picked large caps (Sharpe 1.27), which failed to generalise: on a 30-name universe the same engine underperformed its benchmark (Sharpe 0.86 vs 0.98, 14.2% vs 17.0% annualised, hit-rate 46.6%) (internal build record, RET5 row). An earlier headline "~24%/year" claim from the project’s first phase was classified OWNER-CLAIM/UNVERIFIED in the audit and attributed to roughly 2× leverage on smart-beta over a survivor universe, and a previously documented Sharpe of 1.32 was retracted as optimistic because it omitted financing costs and predated independent concentration enforcement (internal project records).

So the interesting engineering problem was never signal generation. It was building a validation process that could not be fooled by its own operator, then feeding it every data source a retail researcher's budget can reach, and accepting whatever it said. This paper describes that process and its results. The results are negative, and the claim of the paper is that they are trustworthy precisely because of how they were obtained.

## 2. The platform

The trading engine, TradingEngineResearch, is a modular single-process system running a synchronous 13-step decision cycle: data ingestion and point-in-time validation, market-state estimation (HMM regime plus a 7-detector crisis composite), volatility and covariance forecasting (GJR-GARCH/HAR-RV, RMT-denoised covariance), seven signal sleeves, a signal-health gate, feature construction, ML prediction, meta-label admission with an ex-ante cost gate, Black-Litterman optimisation with an exact CVaR linear programme, a pre-trade risk gate, execution planning, execution with transaction-cost analysis, and post-trade learning (`docs/project-control/AS_IS_ARCHITECTURE.md` L11–31; `core/engine/engine.py` L218–307; the CVaR linear programme: `core/engine/optimizer.py` L4, L19). It has three modes, RESEARCH, PAPER-SHADOW and LIVE, with LIVE disabled by default and double-armed: arming requires an explicit `confirm_live=True` and an audit log path, and the default mode is RESEARCH (`core/config.py`).

The safety property that matters for this paper is that the engine is fail-closed against its own research. Under rule SIGNALS-5, a sleeve without a registered validation result is capped at 0.25 weight, tightened to 0.0 in LIVE, so no unvalidated signal can drive real money (`docs/project-control/RISK_AND_DEFECT_REGISTER.md` L33). A single auditable test module proves four lines of defence: unknown mode strings are rejected; a bare `mode=LIVE` assignment is rejected without both arming conditions; the broker factory refuses to build a real-money broker from an unconfirmed or anonymous config, even one constructed so as to bypass validation; and full RESEARCH and PAPER cycles provably never reach a broker, verified with a spy broker and a LIVE positive control (15 test functions, `tests/test_safety_no_live_path.py`).

Every financial event (orders, fills, cash, reconciliations) is recorded in an append-only event ledger in which each event's SHA-256 hash includes the previous event's hash, chaining from a genesis value; mistakes are corrected by appending explicit reversing events, never by editing, and caller-supplied timestamps make replays bit-reproducible (`ops/ledger.py` L1–46). The order state machine treats broker reality honestly: a submission timeout is not a rejection (`SUBMISSION_UNCERTAIN` is a first-class state), a cancel request is not a cancellation, duplicate fills are idempotent no-ops, fills are clamped so filled quantity never exceeds approved quantity, and an unknown broker state blocks resubmission (`execution/order_lifecycle.py` L1–100). A Hypothesis `RuleBasedStateMachine` fuzzes random operation sequences against these invariants (`tests/test_property_order_state.py` L7–26). The suite stood at 1,071 tests passing at the last documented green run on 13 July 2026, with 1,079 collected on 14 July — collection is not a pass count, and the suite has grown since; run `pytest --collect-only` for the current count. CI runs ruff, mypy, pytest with a coverage floor of 80% (baseline 88%) and a gitleaks secret-scan job (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`; `pyproject.toml` L130). The execution path was validated with a real fill on a funded Alpaca paper account: one share of AAPL, filled at $286.08, order round-tripped and auto-flattened (internal session records).

The consequence of all this appears in Section 6: an engine that ran a live paper session flawlessly and, correctly, traded nothing.

## 3. The validation gate

All studies from the learned-combination stage onward share one pipeline. `research/alpha_factory.py` fits a closed-form ridge cross-sectional combiner via purged walk-forward (`learn_signal_weights`, L750–758), and `research/validation.py` is declared the only permitted cross-validation implementation in the project, banning standard k-fold and plain train/test splits from research paths, citing López de Prado (2018), *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*, ch. 7 (`research/validation.py` L1–12).

Promotion is decided by `selection_rule`, an all-or-nothing default-deny gate. It enforces seven checks: mean rank-IC > 0.01 (minimum edge), net-of-cost Sharpe > 0.75, IC stability > 0.60, a deflated-Sharpe proxy > 0.25, the full Deflated Sharpe Ratio ≥ 0.95, zero leakage flags, and no single regime with Sharpe below −0.50; any one failure blocks promotion (`research/validation.py` L401–467). One honest wrinkle: the docstring still says "six conditions" from the original build spec; the seventh, the full DSR test at 0.95, was added when the validator was hardened later, so the gate is best described as the spec's six plus a subsequently added full-DSR check (L406–414 vs body; L45).

The DSR follows Bailey and López de Prado (2014): the Sharpe estimator's variance uses skew and non-excess kurtosis with a T−1 denominator, and the benchmark Sharpe is the expected maximum of N trials, weighted with the Euler–Mascheroni constant (`research/validation.py` L315–357). One simplification, visible in the code: the paper scales that benchmark by the cross-trial variance of the N Sharpe estimates, while this implementation proxies it with the single strategy's standard error, a reasonable choice when the sibling track records were never held in one matrix (L324, L348–357). The function fails closed, returning 0.0 (deny) on degenerate input (L327–347), and its docstring mandates honest counting: `n_trials` is the number of configurations tried during the research, not folds (L330–332). Overfitting probability is computed by Combinatorial Symmetric Cross-Validation after Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado and Zhu (2017) (L360–396). The splitter purges by bar count rather than calendar days, a recorded defect fix: calendar purging under-purges on a business-day index and leaks the label-window tail (L136–178).

A gate is only useful if it provably passes real edges and denies noise, so the gate itself is under test. `tests/test_edge_recovery_proof.py` runs four controls on seeded synthetic data: (A) it recovers a clearly planted edge; (A′) it passes a genuine edge tuned to land *near* the threshold, DSR ≈ 0.965–0.987 with a believable net Sharpe of ~2.2–2.5, proving discrimination rather than saturation; (B) it denies pure noise; and (C) it denies the best of 50 pure-noise configurations selected in-sample, which shows an in-sample Sharpe above 1.5 that would clear a naive gate, while the PBO detector separates it from a persistent edge by a required margin (`tests/test_edge_recovery_proof.py` L165–349). The file also states its own limits: it proves the detector works where ground truth is known by construction, and "the honest prior remains no-easy-alpha" (L40–51). This answers the obvious sceptic's question about a programme that keeps saying no: the gate demonstrably says yes to real edges near its own threshold.

Study 0 established that classic price/volume signals carry no robust alpha in liquid US equities, with the engine's returns attributed to smart-beta, vol targeting and risk management, plus leverage in the higher-risk tiers, rather than alpha (internal week-plan record). One flag: later result documents cite a "461-name price/volume study" as a banked prior, but no primary result artefact for it exists in the repository, only the runner scripts, so it is cited here as a session-recorded prior and the 461 figure is not relied on (`learned_combination_result.md` L79; `scripts/broad_research.py` L1–10). Notably, that script's own docstring flags its survivorship bias: "weak results here are a strong negative; strong results must be discounted" (L8–9).

Singles failed first: no single factor-loading tilt survived deflation, the best being a size tilt at DSR 0.83 under 4-trial deflation (the same file reports 0.73 for the identical measurement under the composite study's 7-trial deflation, a discrepancy explained by trial count and flagged here; `stage_b_ff_loadings_result.md` L15 vs L37). A naive equal-weight composite of six signals was *worse* than the best single signal (Sharpe −0.29). So, is it the combination? A learned ridge combiner over eight point-in-time-safe features decisively beat the naive composite re-run in the same pipeline (DSR 0.467 vs 0.001; net Sharpe 0.92 vs −0.70), with mean IC +0.113, but failed on 84 monthly observations. Is it sample size, then? Breadth alone made things worse: at 473 names without fundamentals, the IC fell from +0.11 to +0.04 and the DSR to 0.254, indicating the fundamentals had carried most of the signal (`learned_combination_result.md` L56–70). Richer free fundamentals across 140 names and 20 years still failed at DSR 0.543, and this study registered a bias-direction argument worth naming: the current-listed universe is biased *in the signal's favour*, so a failure despite the favourable bias is robust (`free_richer_fundamentals_result.md` L38–41). Its EDGAR XBRL bridge had been adversarially reviewed before the run (L18–21), catching a period-mixing bug that alone would have flipped net income by up to ~4× (L42–43), so the failure is a real measurement, not an artefact.

The insider study is treated in Section 5; its headline is a corrected DSR of 0.007, banked under a pre-registered power constraint as "cannot certify a deployable edge" rather than evidence the literature effect is absent (`insider_alpha_result.md` L51–71). The paid Sharadar study then produced "the strongest honest result this project has ever measured", DSR 0.905 with PBO 0.00 on a zero-selection universe verified to contain delisted names (ENRNQ, LEHMQ, WCOEQ, SHLDQ), and the gate still refused it; the result file explicitly bans threshold-chasing, since re-running variants until 0.905 crosses 0.95 is textbook selection bias (`sharadar_alpha_result.md` L28–74). Two small flags: the file states 21,916 names (L4, L22) while its own export verification lists 21,915 tickers (L18–19), and the insider pre-registration's "140-name" prose is corrected by its erratum to a 141-name registered constant, with 140 run after one price download failed (`insider_study_prereg.md` L119–121).

The trial ledger across the programme reads 4 → 7 → 10 → 16 → 8/9 → 16 → 17+d → 23, where the insider count rose from 8 to 9 because a superseded defective run still counts as a look, and the final dev ledger charges 16 full-sample trials plus one dev baseline plus six ladder runs (sources itemised across the study files; e.g. `insider_study_prereg.md` L117–118; `sharadar_dev_log.md` L120). The plan also disclosed, before the paid run, that `n_trials` "likely under-counts the full research search space, so deflation is slightly lenient" (`docs/project-control/DATA_EDGE_PLAN.md` L92–93, written 2026-06-30); the result file, on the day of that run, went further: factor definitions, the ridge spec, windows and the gate itself embed design choices, so "the honest deflation is harsher than 16" (`sharadar_alpha_result.md` L52–54).

## 5. Two artefact hunts

The programme's two best findings were not signals. They were defects in measurements, found by treating every result, including failures, as something to attack.

**The rename join.** The insider study joined SEC Form 4 filings (82 quarters of raw filings, 2006–2026; `insider_study_prereg.md` L59) to prices by as-filed ticker symbol. The pre-registered protocol was review-then-run; the 11 July session ran after only a partial review, a deviation recorded in a dated erratum appended without editing anything above it (`insider_study_prereg.md` L100–111). The full adversarial review on 13 July (a 4-lens workflow, 44 agents, findings verified refute-by-default) then found that the as-filed ticker join had silently lost about 22% of matched rows across issuer renames: GOOG→GOOGL, FB→META, UTX→RTX, PCLN→BKNG and others, with Alphabet effectively absent from the entire sample; separately, 41% of purchase-leg dollar value had been duplicated by multi-owner filing fan-out (`insider_study_prereg.md` L100–116). The corrected run used an audited CIK bridge, evidenced by a 158-pair ticker–CIK audit file (`insider_universe_cik_audit.csv`), recovering 131,872 rows (507,680 → 639,552) including Alphabet's full history and removing ~4,300 pollution rows, such as First Commonwealth filings recorded as "F" inside Ford's history (`insider_alpha_result.md` L24–27, L74–84). The result: "Fixing the measurement made the signal WEAKER — a cleaner zero, not a suppressed edge" (L83). That sentence is the strongest evidence in the repository that the corrections were measurement fixes, not tuning.

**The micro-cap mirage.** The Sharadar development window (data to 2015, under a physically separated dev/confirm split) produced a baseline that looked spectacular: net Sharpe 1.33, DSR 1.000, PBO 0.00. Every other gate criterion passed. The single failure was the humblest: mean rank-IC, at −0.0155 against a threshold of +0.01 (`sharadar_dev_log.md` L8–27). Chasing that one dissenting number, an exact replication of the gate arithmetic showed the Sharpe lived entirely in untradeable micro-caps: the top decile alone was more than 100% of P&L, the least-liquid dollar-volume quintile carried 112.7% of gross P&L, and the decile's median stock traded at $3.61 with median trailing-63-day dollar volume of $51,500 (L34–45). The kill-shots followed: capping monthly returns at ±100% flipped the Sharpe from +1.33 to −0.43; a single name-month, ILXRQ in May 2009, up 9,900% on zero volume, was 13% of total P&L (L46–49). Data QA confirmed both monster prints as artefacts: ILXRQ was an indicative-quote reprice on a bankrupt shell, unrealisable by construction, and AWHL's +33,650% was an unadjusted reverse split, a genuine vendor data bug; the forward-return cap became standard equipment thereafter (L122–134, entry 3b).

The moral is precise. The two sophisticated overfitting statistics, DSR 1.000 and PBO 0.00, both waved the mirage through, because it was not overfitting; it was a construction earning fictional returns in stocks that cannot be traded. The plain Spearman rank criterion caught it, and because Spearman correlation is invariant to monotone transforms, no cosmetic rescaling of the signal could have gamed it (`sharadar_dev_log.md` L50–54). The full-sample result's addendum downgraded the near-miss accordingly: "the negative rank-IC (−0.037) was the honest tell, and the rank-IC gate criterion was right" (`sharadar_alpha_result.md` L88–90). Gates need orthogonal criteria; the cheap one saved the expensive ones.

A six-run liquidity ladder then measured what remained inside tradable universes (top-500/1000/1500 and a $5M dollar-volume floor, capped returns from the second run onward, 10 and 20 bps costs). Rank-IC turned genuinely positive, +0.010 to +0.018, and net Sharpe was ≤ 0 in every variant, reaching −0.28 at 20 bps; the learned ridge underperformed a naive equal-weight composite in liquid universes, and a ceiling analysis put the best case at ~+0.2–0.4 net Sharpe against the 0.75 gate (`sharadar_dev_log.md` L78–118). The banked recommendation was to fold rather than spend the confirmation shot.

## 6. What survived

Three things survived the programme, and none of them is alpha.

First, the engine's honest baseline. On its default configuration (8 large caps, monthly, 2016–2024, net of costs including financing on leverage) it returns 18.4% annualised, Sharpe 1.15, maximum drawdown 17.1%, against an equal-weight buy-and-hold benchmark at 18.0% and Sharpe 1.12 (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, Status section). The project's documents describe this as market-beta with cost-honest drawdown control, not alpha; the edge over passive is thin, and the hand-picked 8-survivor universe is a disclosed remaining optimism. Second, that baseline is data-source-proof: rerun on the paid survivorship-free data with only the price source changed, it reproduced to two decimal places (18.37%/1.15/17.09% vs 18.4%/1.15/17.1%), with a daily-return correlation of 1.000000 on the overlapping sample (measured against the then-committed price fixture, which has since been replaced with synthetic data for licence reasons), run twice bit-identically under a fixed seed (`sharadar_dev_log.md` entry 4, L136–157; `scripts/backtest_real_sharadar.py`). A deliberately modest headline that replicates exactly across two independent data vendors is worth more than a spectacular one that does not.

Third, a real measurement of a real edge that cannot be used. Clean, survivorship-free fundamentals do order next-month returns in tradable US equities, at rank-IC around +0.013 on the development window. The economics kill it: a monthly linear long-short construction turns over too much for an edge this thin, and moving costs from 10 to 20 bps alone removes roughly 0.23 of Sharpe (`sharadar_dev_log.md` L92–118). This is the quantitative core of the retail problem: the residual edges in free-tier data are real, small, and priced away by the costs a retail account actually pays. Consistent with all of this, the engine's fail-closed design produced the correct terminal behaviour: a live-mode paper session (Alpaca) ran all 13 pipeline steps, cycle count incrementing, and placed zero orders, because every sleeve was unvalidated and therefore weighted zero, with the meta-labeller and sizing steps then confirming the empty book. The session record describes it as "working as designed, not a bug" (internal session records).

## 7. Discussion

What separates this programme from p-hacking with extra paperwork is a set of mechanisms, each of which cost something at the time it was applied.

Pre-registration with teeth: the insider study fixed its hypothesis, five features, universe, dates, gate and any-fail-is-final decision rule before any real-data run, anchored to published effect sizes of ~2–5%/yr (Lakonishok and Lee 2001; Jeng, Metrick and Zeckhauser 2003; Cohen, Malloy and Pomorski 2012) and explicitly framed as "NOT a 30%/yr candidate" (`insider_study_prereg.md` L1–97). Errata are appended, dated, and self-incriminating; nothing above them is edited. Trial accounting is honest to the point of masochism: a superseded defective run raised the trial count, and the undercount of the true search space was disclosed before the definitive run rather than after. A power analysis registered before the corrected insider rerun capped what the failure could mean, so the banked verdict is "cannot certify a deployable edge", not "the effect is absent" (`insider_alpha_result.md` L51–71). Bias direction was registered a priori, so a favourable bias made failures more credible instead of successes. Reviews ran before runs where the protocol held, and where it did not, the deviation itself was documented. And the terminal act of the programme was declining to spend a pre-registered confirmation shot it was entitled to fire, because no model could pass the development-side gate; the confirmation window remains unconsumed, and any future attempt requires a new pre-registration with cumulative trial accounting (`sharadar_confirmatory_prereg.md` L94–106). Folding on schedule, rather than searching until something crosses a line, is the discipline the whole apparatus exists to enforce.

What did the ~£60 buy? Not alpha. It bought the ability to distinguish "no edge in my data" from "no edge": a universe with its corpses still in it, the discovery of a genuine vendor data artefact (the AWHL unadjusted reverse split), the micro-cap diagnosis, and the clean replication of the engine baseline. That is a fair return on a month's subscription (internal session records). What professional infrastructure buys, and retail cannot, is visible in this programme's limitations: modelled delisting returns (the registered limitation, `docs/project-control/DATA_EDGE_PLAN.md` L94–95), point-in-time universes at every date rather than a bias-direction argument (`learned_combination_result.md` L42–46), and richer-than-monthly data plus the execution machinery that makes thin edges harvestable at scale (`sharadar_dev_log.md` L96–99). The validation machinery itself proved to be the most reusable output; it was extracted into a standalone backtest-overfitting checker maintained as a separate private project, scoped to published statistics only.

The multi-agent adversarial review workflow deserves a note, since it found both artefacts in Section 5. Findings were verified refute-by-default, meaning each claimed defect had to survive an active attempt to disprove it before being acted on; recorded instances include the insider review (44 agents) and earlier reviews whose confirmed/refuted splits are logged in the specs (`docs/project-control/specs/2026-06-30-held-book-reconciliation-design.md` L180–182). Scepticism was applied to the sceptics.

## 8. How this was built

This project was built with AI assistance, and it would be dishonest — and would miss the methodological point — to present it otherwise. I directed the programme: I set the question, chose and enforced the standards (the default-deny gate, pre-registration, review-before-run, honest trial accounting), made every go/no-go decision (funding the paid data, accepting the fold, closing the programme unfired), and adjudicated what survived review. AI coding agents did most of the hands-on work: they wrote code and tests, ran the studies, and were also deployed *against* their own output — multi-agent adversarial reviews (forty-four agents in the largest, with every finding challenged by independent sceptic agents before acceptance) whose job was to refute, not to confirm.

Both of the defects in Section 5 were introduced by AI-written code and caught by AI-driven review operating under a process designed so that neither the human nor the machines could quietly pass their own work. That is the honest lesson of building this way in 2026: when agents do the implementation, the human contribution shifts almost entirely to what this paper is about — specifying what would count as evidence, building the machinery that cannot be sweet-talked, and having the discipline to accept its answer. Every quantitative claim in this document was verified against the repository by independent fact-checking passes before publication.

## 9. Conclusion

The question was whether a disciplined retail researcher can find deployable equity alpha in public data. The answer, measured across price/volume signals, factor loadings, EDGAR fundamentals, Form 4 insider filings and a paid survivorship-free fundamentals sample, is no: every study is banked NOT-DEPLOYABLE, and the near-misses dissolved under diagnosis into artefacts of measurement rather than suppressed edges (internal session records). The residual truth is a rank-IC of roughly +0.013 in clean fundamentals, measured on the development window: real, reproducible, and unmonetisable by this construction at honest retail costs.

I regard this as a successful outcome. The system that would deploy an edge exists, is tested to over a thousand collected tests (1,079 at the time of writing) including a proof that its own gate discriminates near threshold, and correctly trades nothing. The next candidates are already mapped, FINRA short interest (2018+), SEC 13F holdings (2013Q2+) and fails-to-deliver data (2004+), each requiring its own pre-registration and review-before-run protocol. Higher-frequency or alternative data might change the answer; nothing in this programme entitles me to assume it will. What the programme does entitle me to say is narrower and, I think, more valuable: when a result finally does pass this gate, there will be good reasons to believe it.
